The gap is the finding. Filtering a feature window on when a trip was assigned rather than when it finished buys 0.92 minutes of MAE — more than the honest model's entire lift over the baseline — and none of it survives production, because at prediction time those trips had not finished and one of them is the trip being predicted. Dropping the window altogether gaps only 0.09 minutes: small enough to dismiss as noise, wrong for exactly the same reason, and the shape a GROUP BY written without a date filter actually takes.

How it is built

Trips come from Dispatch's gold table, where assigned_at is the instant a prediction was due and tat_minutes only exists once the trip ends. Features are declared once and retrieved two ways — point-in-time for training, by entity for serving — and a test replays a day asserting the two paths return identical values for every trip.
